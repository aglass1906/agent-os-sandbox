# The Keeper of the Lantern

Old Wren of Bramble Hollow, apprentice to no one and keeper of the town's only lantern, stood alone at the top of the crooked watchtower while dusk came down and swallowed the valley, the rough stone warm against his calloused palms and the whole of Bramble Hollow unspooled below him in wattle-and-daub cottages, their chimney smoke going soft against the bruise-colored sky as the single crooked lane folded itself up like a hen settling on her eggs; and when a golden moth the size of his palm drifted up out of the blue hour and settled on his thumb as if it had known him for years, he did not swat it away but only smiled, for he had lit that lantern every evening for forty years and knew that a man who tends a light is too often alone in the dark, and up here, with the first star rising over the hill like a coin dropped into a well, it felt almost like a small, kind blessing to have someone stay a while.
