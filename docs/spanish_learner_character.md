## 1. Scope & Purpose

This document defines the **canonical protagonist** of the Hub's Spanish
language-learning experience: the primary character whose journey players
inhabit across the vocabulary, audio-comprehension, and reading games. It
establishes the character's identity, background, personality foundations, and a
**four-stage growth arc**.

Every Spanish feature in the Hub — Word Rain's *Lluvia de Palabras*, reading
passages such as `historia_espanol.md`, and future comprehension games — is
written from this character's point of view and must respect the stage he is in.
Stage, not age, drives difficulty, pacing, and vocabulary themes.

## 2. Character Identity & Background

### 2.1 Identity snapshot

| Field | Canon |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | Leo Álvarez |
| **Age at journey start** | 10 years old |
| **Age range across the full arc** | 10–12 years old |
| **Language status** | Native English speaker (effectively monolingual at start) |
| **Target language** | Spanish (neutral Latin American Spanish) |
| **Hometown** | Austin, Texas, USA |
| **Household** | Mother (third-grade teacher), father (restaurant owner), one older sister, and a family cat of his own named Chispas |
| **Signature items** | A spiral notebook ("mi cuaderno"), a worn stress ball, and a hand-me-down tablet running *Lluvia de Palabras* |
| **Beloved figure** | Abuela María (owner of Tomás the cat, protagonist of `historia_espanol.md`) |

### 2.2 Background narrative

Leo Álvarez is a ten-year-old from Austin. Born into a household where Spanish
rattles around the margins — menus, radio songs, his neighbors' *"¿Cómo estás?"*
— but no one sits down and *talks* to him in it. His mother sprinkles casual
phrases into daily life; his father knows a dozen words and is proud of them; his
older sister quit Spanish class in eighth grade and still regrets it. Spanish is
*a sound around him*, never *a conversation with him*.

That changes the summer he turns ten, after his abuela María moves back to
Guadalajara to care for her own mother. Leo visits twice a year. He loves her
fiercely, but the visits are full of well-intentioned pauses: María speaks
beautifully, warmly, **fully** — in Spanish. Leo laughs, guesses, and answers in
single English words. He hates the guessing and the pauses. He cannot tell her
about his day the way she tells him about hers.

The journey begins when his mother hands him the old tablet with *Lluvia de
Palabras* on it and he sets himself a promise, one that anchors the entire arc:

> *"By the time Abuela comes for Christmas, I want to read her the story about
> her cat, El gato de María — out loud, in her own language."*

Every stage below exists to earn that promise.

## 3. Personality Foundations

Leo is built from seven **core traits**, each deliberately chosen because it
**actively supports the language-learning journey** (rather than merely being
"nice"). One trait is a **shadow trait** — a flaw that arcs and grows into
strength across the four stages.

### 3.1 Core trait table

| Trait | How it supports the language journey | In-game expression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Curiosity** | Drives word-hunting and question-asking; a new word is a present, not a task. | Exploring new Word Rain lexical bands; tapping "Listen" to re-hear a word. |
| **Resilience (grit)** | A wrong pick is data, not a verdict; a missed word teaches, a lost life reminds. | Streak resets and `lives` loss are framed as pit stops, never failures. |
| **Risk-taking** | Guesses aloud even when unsure; courage beats correctness early. | Free-text answers are always rewarded for effort, then corrected gently. |
| **Playfulness** | Treats accent, rhythm, and tone as music; repetition is a game, not drills. | Shadowing TTS pronunciation in Word Rain's prompt. |
| **Pattern sense** | Spots cognates, roots, and gender endings early. | Bonus recognition cues: *✦* for spotting *"la"/"el"* or *"-ción"/"-dad"* patterns. |
| **Patience** | Trusts the slow climb; fluency is a season, not a sprint. | Mirrors the gradual difficulty escalation across lexical bands. |
| **Connection** | The emotional engine — learning is *for Abuela María*, never for a grade. | All story passages (`historia_espanol.md`) center on her world. |

### 3.2 Shadow trait: shame about his accent

Leo's arc-defining flaw is **productive perfectionism**: he refuses to say
Spanish words aloud because he believes his accent sounds *"dumb."* He absorbs
perfectly, but freezes when it is his turn to speak. This shadow is the engine of
his growth — each stage converts a little more fear into courage, until by Stage 4
he is *proud* of his accent because it sounds like *him*. The shadow trait is
what separates a character arc from a checklist.

## 4. The Four Growth Stages

Each stage is a discrete, reachable chapter with its own narrative name,
CEFR-aligned target, emotional arc, and a **stage-exit checkpoint** that must be
passed before the next stage feels earned.

| Stage | Narrative name | Approx. age | CEFR target | Word Rain band (§7) | Emotional arc |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Los Primeros Pasos | 10 | A1 (low) | Band 0 | Hesitation → first true words |
| **2** | La Curiosidad Creciente | 10–11 | A1–A2 | Band 1 | Play → confidence |
| **3** | La Confianza | 11 | A2–B1 | Band 2 | Confidence → independence |
| **4** | La Comunidad | 11–12 | B1+ | Band 3 | Independence → belonging |

### 4.1 Stage 1 — *Los Primeros Pasos* (First Steps)

**Age:** 10. **CEFR target:** A1 low. **Word Rain band:** 0.

**Narrative frame.** The summer before fifth grade. Leo gets the tablet and makes
his promise about Christmas. He is frightened of the words and terrified of his
own voice. He starts with his notebook and the falling words game on the easiest
band: greetings, family, food, the cat Tomás.

**Milestones.** Salvage ten core words with comprehension, not memorization:
*hola, gracias, gato, casa, agua, leche, grande, pequeño, sí, no.* Correct picks
start flowing. He reads his first whole sentence: *"María tiene un gato."*

**Vocabulary theme.** Self + home: family, animals, food, basic adjectives.

**Trait bloom.** **Curiosity** flowers; **patience** begins. The shadow trait
shows its teeth: he plays, he collects, but he will not *speak*.

**Setback & recovery.** A streak broken at five by a missed word makes him want
to quit. His mother normalizes it — "you fell, you fell in Spanish, that's the
falling game's job" — and he restarts. Resilience is seeded.

**Aha moment.** On the last day of summer he writes to Abuela María in his
best handwriting: *"Quiero aprender español para ti, Abuela."* It is written, not
spoken — but it is his.

**Stage-exit checkpoint.** Complete Band 0 of *Lluvia de Palabras* with at least
30 cumulative correct picks and demonstrate comprehension of simple present-tense
phrases around family vocabulary.

### 4.2 Stage 2 — *La Curiosidad Creciente* (Growing Curiosity)

**Age:** 10–11. **CEFR target:** A1–A2. **Word Rain band:** 1.

**Narrative frame.** Autumn of fifth grade. The tablet game starts speaking new
bands — colors, weather, days, numbers, the park where Tomás chases leaves. Leo
starts *noticing* Spanish everywhere: the taco shop menu, a soccer chant, the
lyrics of a song he hums. He becomes a field linguist, notebook in hand, tagging
cognates: *música, parque, estación.*

**Milestones.** Reaches the A2 threshold of ~120 understood words. Can follow
short spoken instructions. Spots his first false friend and loves it
(*embarazada* = not "embarrassed").

**Vocabulary theme.** The world around him: places, weather, colors, numbers,
actions; the park setting of `historia_espanol.md`.

**Trait bloom.** **Playfulness** and **pattern sense** ignite. He plays with
sound, repeats words to feel the rhythm, and starts correcting the TTS voice's
pace in his head. The shadow trait thaws: he whispers words to Chispas the cat,
who never judges his accent.

**Setback & recovery.** At a family dinner he says *"Estoy embarazado"* intending
"embarrassed" — a small eruption of laughter. It crushes him for a week. His
sister, the regretful quitter, tells him the truth: "Everyone I know who quit
Spanish quit because one wrong word embarrassed them. You just kept yours." He
launches back in with the false-friend list in his notebook.

**Aha moment.** Mid-December he reads the whole of *El gato de María* silently
and understands it. He does not tell anyone yet.

**Stage-exit checkpoint.** Pass Band 1 with a live streak of 10+, and retell a
five-sentence simplified version of *El gato de María* from memory in English —
proving comprehension, not translation.

### 4.3 Stage 3 — *La Confianza* (Confidence)

**Age:** 11. **CEFR target:** A2–B1. **Word Rain band:** 2.

**Narrative frame.** Christmas visit to Guadalajara. Leo arrives armed with a
notebook, a plan, and a frozen tongue. For two days he hides behind his mother.
Then Abuela María sets two mugs of hot chocolate on the table and says, slowly
and warmly: *"Leo, cuéntame de tu vida."* And he does — haltingly, with hand
gestures, in mix-and-match Spanish that makes everyone at the table grin and
help. The shadow trait is finally faced head-on.

**Milestones.** Holds a ten-minute conversation in Spanish. Learns that fluency
is not correctness. Produces his first past tense stories ("ayer caminamos en el
parque"). Volume of understanding now outpaces vocabulary recall, which feels
exactly like the game's Bands.

**Vocabulary theme.** Narrative and relationship: feelings, storytelling, past
actions, the family table.

**Trait bloom.** **Risk-taking** and **connection** peak. He speaks despite the
accent he once feared, and María's joy is a bigger reward than any score.

**Setback & recovery.** On New Year's Eve he freezes mid-story, loses the thread
in front of the whole family, and flees to the kitchen. María follows and tells
him the story's real ending: *"Hijo, yo llevo sesenta años aprendiendo español y
todavía tropiezo. Tropezar es la prueba de que vas andando."* He returns and
finishes the story with everyone cheering the wrong-word he made.

**Aha moment.** Abuela María says, *"Tu español tiene música,"* — the first time
anyone has praised his *voice*, not his vocabulary.

**Stage-exit checkpoint.** Pass Band 2 and deliver an unscripted 1-minute
monologue about a real memory, end to end, in Spanish, in front of at least one
Spanish speaker.

### 4.4 Stage 4 — *La Comunidad* (Community / Fluency)

**Age:** 11–12. **CEFR target:** B1+. **Word Rain band:** 3.

**Narrative frame.** Spring and the year that follows. Leo is no longer "the kid
learning Spanish" — he is simply *Leo, who speaks Spanish*. He is invited to the
summer neighborhood Spanish reading circle. He writes his own five-sentence story
in the style of *El gato de María*, starring Chispas the cat. He becomes the 
bridge in his own household, teaching his father one new word a week and 
translating the neighbor's greetings so his sister finally talks to them.

**Milestones.** Comfortably handles Band 3 audio comprehension. Produces
connected narrative in the past and future. Can read a short story and retell it
in Spanish. Understands culture as part of language: *sobremesa, ahorita, querer
bien.*

**Vocabulary theme.** Community and identity: months, ambitions, humor, idioms,
family lore.

**Trait bloom.** **Connection** becomes the dominant trait and selflessly
redirects outward: he no longer learns *for* María — he learns *with* and *for
others*. The shadow trait has fully inverted into **proud-playful identity**: his
accent is no longer wrong, it is *his*.

**Setback & recovery.** A summer reading-circle elder gently corrects his *casa*
and *aves* pronunciation in front of everyone. Old Leo would have bolted. New Leo
laughs, repeats it, and thanks him — and reads the line again. Growth is
demonstrated by how a setback is metabolized, not by the absence of setbacks.

**Aha moment.** At the Christmas display table the following year he reads
*El gato de María* aloud from memory — his one-year promise, kept — and Abuela
María, Tomás in her lap, cries happy tears. Then she hands him a blank notebook
and says, *"Ahora tú escribes la siguiente historia."*

**Stage-exit checkpoint.** Pass Band 3; write, rehearse, and read aloud an
original five-sentence Spanish story in a shared setting; and complete one act of
generational teaching (teaching another family member a week's worth of Spanish).

## 5. Stage-to-Game Mapping

| Stage | Word Rain band (§7) | Hub games & materials in canon | Sample vocabulary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Primeros Pasos | Band 0 | *Lluvia de Palabras* easiest band; `historia_espanol.md` read silently | gato, casa, leche, grande, hola, sí |
| 2 — Curiosidad Creciente | Band 1 | *Lluvia de Palabras*; park scenes of `historia_espanol.md`; Memory Match with picture-word pairs | parque, pájaro, hoja, mañana, tarde |
| 3 — La Confianza | Band 2 | *Lluvia de Palabras*; spoken retelling of `historia_espanol.md` | caminar, leer, mirar, ayer, después |
| 4 — La Comunidad | Band 3 | *Lluvia de Palabras* fast bands; writing circle; original Chispas stories | meses, querer, escribir, reír, el año |

Difficulty and theme are stage-bound: a Stage 1 player should never meet Stage 4
idioms, and a Stage 4 scene should never talk down at Stage 1 level. Stage
gating is a **content rule** shared by all Spanish features in the Hub.

## 6. Continuity & Canon

- `historia_espanol.md` ( *El gato de María* ) is the **anchor text** of the
  journey: Maria and Tomás are canon, and the promise "read the cat story to
  Abuela" bookends Stage 1 → Stage 4.
- María is canonically Leo's **abuela**; her warm, simple story style sets the
  register of every in-world passage.
- Chispas (Leo's own cat) is introduced as a parallel to Tomás and appears in
  the Stage 4 original-story milestone.
- Neutral Latin American Spanish is canonical for all Hub Spanish text and TTS
  (consistent with `word_rain_design.md` speech settings).

## 7. Consistency & Quality Criteria

Per `AGENTS.md` verification culture, any content written against this
framework must pass:

1. **Stage discipline** — vocabulary, TTS rate, and themes match the character's
   current stage table (§4), never ahead or behind.
2. **Trait integrity** — every scene advances at least one core trait or
   transforms the shadow trait; no scene is trait-neutral filler.
3. **Canon continuity** — María, Tomás, and Chispas appearances are consistent
   with §6; no contradictions with `historia_espanol.md`.
4. **Emotional arc evidence** — each stage's *aha moment* and *setback &
   recovery* are present or referenced; growth is demonstrated through how
   setbacks are metabolized, not through flawless success.
5. **Representation & accessibility** — Leo's experience is conveyed through
   visible text and audio-optional channels, honoring the Hub's accessibility
   policy; humor is gentle, never mocking a learner's accent.

## 8. Out-of-Scope / Future Work

- Character **visual design** (sprites, palette) is intentionally out of scope
  for this authoring stage and deferred until an art pass.
- Leo's **older sister** and **father** are secondary characters with arcs that
  may be authored separately; only Leo's arc is canonical here.
- A fifth "Mastery / Mentor" stage is deliberately **not** defined: the four
  stages above are the canonical arc, keeping the learner's growth bounded and
  measurable.
